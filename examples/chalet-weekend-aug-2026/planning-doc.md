# Friday

## BBQ Shish Taouk
**Serves 26 — store-bought marinated**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Marinated chicken taouk, cubed on skewers | 2.5 kg |
| Toum, for dipping | from the 2.5 kg |

1. If not pre-skewered, thread onto ~32 skewers.
2. Grill over medium-high 10–12 min, turning, until charred and cooked through
   (internal 74 °C / 165 °F).
3. Rest 5 min. Serve with toum for dipping — not lemon.

## Kafta
**Serves 26 — store-bought marinated**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Marinated kafta, formed on skewers | 2.5 kg |

1. If not pre-formed, mould onto flat skewers into logs of ~80 g.
2. Grill 4–5 min per side over medium-high until browned through.
3. Rest before serving.

## Vegan BBQ Oyster Mushroom "Chicken"
**Serves 7**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Oyster mushrooms, torn into strips | 1.2 kg |
| Olive oil | 4 tbsp |
| Smoked paprika | 1 tbsp |
| Garlic powder | 1 tbsp |
| Onion powder | 2 tsp |
| Vegan BBQ sauce | 250 ml |
| Soy sauce or tamari | 2 tbsp |
| Maple syrup | 1 tbsp |
| Salt & pepper | to taste |

1. Toss mushrooms with oil, smoked paprika, garlic and onion powder, salt, pepper.
2. Roast at 220 °C for 15 min, or grill in a basket until edges crisp.
3. Whisk BBQ sauce, tamari and maple. Toss the mushrooms in it.
4. Back on the heat 3–5 min to caramelise. Extra glaze on the side.

## Fries
**Serves 26 — shared, vegan-friendly**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Frozen fries | 1 large bag (~2.25 kg) |
| Salt | to taste |

**Run the two air fryers and the oven in parallel, not in sequence.**

1. **Air fryers** take ~600 g each — a half-full basket, shaken at halfway.
   200 °C, 15–18 min. That's ~1.2 kg, out first and crispest.
2. **Oven** takes the remaining ~1 kg on one sheet pan at 220 °C, ~25 min.
3. Salt everything the second it comes out.

> **Don't do two air-fryer rounds back to back.** Batch one goes cold while
> batch two cooks, and half the table eats warm fries. Running all three at once
> lands everything within about five minutes.
>
> **Watch the breaker.** Two air fryers are roughly 3000 W between them. On one
> chalet circuit, with a kettle or coffee maker going, that trips. Put them on
> different outlets — ideally different rooms.
>
> No oil needed. Frozen fries are already par-fried.

# Sunday

## Sniyet Djej w Batata — Teta's Version
**Lebanese roast chicken & sliced potatoes · Serves 26 · boneless**

Thin overlapping potato slices, whole garlic cloves, sliced onion, seven spice,
olive oil, and enough lemon juice that it pools in the pan. Golden, not red.
No grain underneath.

> **The four things that make it taste like hers:**
> 1. **Slice, don't wedge.** ~5 mm rounds laid overlapping. The buried slices go
>    soft and soaked; the exposed edges catch and darken. Wedges only ever do one
>    or the other.
> 2. **Serve it wet.** ~2 cm of lemon, oil and water in the base, and it comes out
>    with juices still pooling — spooned over the potatoes, mopped with bread.
>    Not reduced to a glaze.
> 3. **Whole garlic cloves, not just minced.** Scattered between the slices they
>    go sweet and soft, almost confit. The minced garlic in the marinade does a
>    different job.
> 4. **Covered most of the way, uncovered only at the end.** Long enough to brown
>    the chicken, not long enough to cook the pan dry.

### Chicken & marinade
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Boneless chicken thighs, **skin-on if available** | 5.5 kg |
| Olive oil | 250 ml |
| Garlic, minced | 18 cloves |
| Lemons | 7 for the marinade (plus 6 for the tray, 4 for serving) |
| Seven spice (sabaa baharat) | 4 tbsp |
| Fine salt | 3½ tbsp |
| Black pepper | 1 tbsp |

### The tray
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Potatoes, sliced ~5 mm, skin on | 7 kg |
| Yellow onions, sliced thin | 6 large |
| Garlic cloves, whole or roughly crushed | ~30 |
| Olive oil | 250 ml |
| Lemon juice | ~300 ml (6 lemons) |
| Water | 900 ml — about 2 cm in the base |
| Fine salt | 2½ tbsp |
| Seven spice | 2 tbsp |

### To finish
Sumac (4 tbsp) · parsley, torn (2 bunches) · lemon wedges · toum · pita
**Bread on the table** — the juices are the point.

### Cook-day timeline — anchored to a 3:00 PM meal

| Time | Do this |
|---|---|
| **1:00 PM** | Preheat every oven to 200 °C / 400 °F. Slice the potatoes ~5 mm and the onions thin. |
| **1:15 PM** | Squeeze 7 lemons over the chicken, massage through the bags, leave at room temperature. |
| **1:25 PM** | Layer the pans: potato slices **overlapping**, onion scattered through, whole garlic cloves tucked between. Oil, salt and seven spice over the top. Pour in the lemon juice and water — **about 2 cm of liquid in the base.** |
| **1:35 PM** | Into the ovens, covered tightly with foil. |
| **1:55 PM** | Lift the foil, lay the chicken on top in a **single layer, skin-side up**, pour all the marinade over. **Do not stir the potatoes** — the layering is the whole point. **Foil back on.** |
| **2:20 PM** | **Uncover** — this is the only uncovered stretch, and it's for colour on the chicken, not for reducing the pan. |
| **2:40 PM** | Check: **74 °C / 165 °F** internal, potatoes tender, chicken browned. There should still be a proper layer of juices in the base. If the chicken is browning too fast, foil back on. |
| **2:45 PM** | Out. Rest 10 min, loosely tented. |
| **2:55 PM** | Sumac, torn parsley, lemon wedges around the edges. |
| **3:00 PM** | Serve, spooning pan juices over. Toum in two bowls. |

> **Set a phone alarm for 1:55 PM in every chalet.** That's the step that goes
> wrong when people are slow-moving after Saturday night.

> **Name the cooks on Friday, not Sunday.** A 1:00 PM start means someone is
> committed by lunchtime the morning after a party. Pick two people now, one per
> oven cluster, and tell them.

> **Don't reduce it at the end.** The instinct on seeing liquid in the tray is to
> leave it in longer. Resist that — pull it while there's still a proper layer of
> juices. That's the dish.

> **This version solves the oven problem.** About 70 minutes total against 80 for
> dry wedges, and it's far more forgiving: a wet tray doesn't punish you if the
> ovens run cold, the timing slips, or it sits fifteen minutes before everyone
> sits down. You no longer need to par-cook anything at home.
>
> It also travels and reheats far better if you do cook ahead — the pan liquid
> protects the potatoes where dry roasted wedges go grainy. Hold back the sumac,
> parsley, lemon and toum until serving either way.

## Vegan Stir Fry & Crispy Tofu
**Serves 7**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Firm tofu, pressed and cubed | 900 g |
| Cornstarch | 4 tbsp |
| Neutral oil | 7 tbsp (4 tofu, 3 veg) |
| Broccoli florets | 400 g |
| Bell peppers, sliced | 3 |
| Carrots, sliced | 3 |
| Snap peas | 300 g |
| Mushrooms, sliced | 300 g |
| Garlic, minced | 6 cloves |
| Ginger, minced | 2 tbsp |
| Soy sauce | 100 ml |
| Sesame oil | 2 tbsp |
| Rice vinegar | 2 tbsp |
| Maple syrup | 2 tbsp |
| Rice, uncooked | 500 g (1 kg bag is plenty) |
| Sesame seeds & green onion | garnish |

1. Toss tofu in cornstarch; pan- or air-fry until golden and crisp. Set aside.
2. Stir-fry garlic and ginger 30 seconds in a hot wok.
3. Add vegetables by density — carrots, broccoli, peppers, mushrooms, snap peas.
   Stir-fry 5–7 min until crisp-tender.
4. Whisk soy, sesame oil, vinegar and maple; pour over and toss.
5. Fold in the tofu, garnish, serve over rice.

> Uses the stovetop while the chicken has the ovens — no conflict.

> **Coffee stays.** "Everything else" clearly wasn't meant to include it.

### Vegan breakfast — one line item, don't cut it
**Croissants contain butter and eggs are eggs.** That leaves your 7 vegan guests
with manakish and jam — which is genuinely fine as a breakfast, since the
manakish are the main event anyway.

The one thing worth keeping is **oat milk (4 L, $14)**. Without it they drink
black coffee for three mornings, and that's a poor trade for fourteen dollars.

### The manakish plan
**5 dozen za'atar — 60 pieces.** Across Saturday, Sunday and Monday that's
about 20 a morning for 33 people, which works alongside eggs, fruit and labneh.

Confirm the by-the-dozen price when you phone it in — za'atar bought in sixes
should come well under the single-piece price.

**All-za'atar has a quiet advantage:** it's vegan. Your 7 vegan guests can eat
the same breakfast as everyone else, which the original spread didn't allow.

**No fridge, no freezer.** There's nothing perishable in a za'atar manoushe —
dough, olive oil, za'atar. Sealed at room temperature they hold three days
comfortably, which covers Saturday through Monday. The oil helps.

**Staling is the only enemy, and it's about air, not warmth.** Keep the stacks
tightly bagged or wrapped from the moment you leave the bakery. An open bag on a
counter goes dry and brittle by Sunday.

**Oven, and it beats the air fryers here.** You're warming about 20 a morning
for 33 people, and an oven takes 10–12 at a time against 4 in a fryer basket.
180 °C:

- **Wrapped in foil, in a stack — 8–10 min.** Soft, pliable, steamy. The way
  they're eaten in Lebanon.
- **Spread flat on sheet pans — 5 min.** Crisper base, drier edges.

Never microwave them — the dough goes leathery. Save the air fryers for the
Friday fries.

> Buy two-thirds of the avocados firm and one-third ripe, or they all go at once.
> Apples and whole pineapple keep for the entire stay without fridge space —
> that's the main reason they beat berries on a four-day trip.
>
> **60 eggs across three mornings is about 0.6 per person.** Treat eggs as a
> Saturday and Sunday thing rather than a daily default.

## The recipes

**Aperol spritz** — 3 parts Prosecco, 2 parts Aperol, splash of soda, over ice
with an orange slice.

**Tequila-kombucha** — 2 oz blanco, 4 oz ginger or citrus kombucha, squeeze of
lime, over ice.

**Arak** — 1 part arak, 2 parts cold water, **then** ice. In that order.

> **The arak order matters and people get it wrong.** Pour the arak, add the
> water — it turns milky white, which is the point — and add ice **last**. Drop
> ice into neat arak and the anise oils crystallise on the surface of the cubes
> and the drink is ruined. Water first, always.
>
> Arak belongs on the Friday table more than anywhere else: mezze, grilled meat,
> arak is the classic combination. Serve it in small glasses, topped up often,
> not in a tumbler.

## Ice is the thing that actually runs out

Sixty kilos gets you through Friday and Saturday. **Plan a restock Saturday** at
the store near the chalets — 100 spritz served over full glasses of ice eats
through it faster than anyone expects, and there's no fixing it at 10 PM.

Check whether the chalets have icemakers before you buy; if two do, you need
much less.

## Chalet Logistics

- **Four pans will not fit in one oven.** Split across 2–3 chalets, one person
  per oven.
- Preheat everything at the same time. Chalet ovens run cold and slow.
- **Don't crowd the chicken.** Overlapping pieces steam instead of roasting.
- **Both quinoa salads go out Saturday**, alongside the tacos. Keep them sealed
  and cold until then. Whatever's left works as late-night grazing and again on
  Sunday — a cold grain salad holds fine once opened, and it's vegan as bought.
- **Name the Sunday cooks on Friday.** A 1:00 PM start means someone is committed
  by lunchtime the morning after a party. Pick two, one per oven cluster.
- Fridge space is the hidden constraint: 13.5 kg of meat, 4 kg of yogurt and
  7 kg of dips. Map out which chalet fridge holds what before you unload.
- **Two air fryers on one circuit will trip a breaker.** Separate outlets,
  ideally separate rooms.
- The manakish need counter space, not fridge or freezer — keep the stacks
  bagged and out of the way.

## What Changed From the Original

- **Breakfast quantities were never multiplied for four mornings** in the old
  consolidated list — every figure was the per-day number. Fixed, and trimmed to
  budget.
- **Sunday switched to the Lebanese version.** Boneless, seven spice, potatoes
  roasted in the chicken fat. Sour cream and chives dropped.
- **Lemons 8 → 18, sumac 3 tbsp → a full jar, onions 3 → 3 kg, olive oil → 3 L.**
  All were short.
- **Seven spice added** — wasn't on the list at all.
- **Toum 1 kg → 2 kg**, now covering two nights.
- **Vegan breakfast items added.** Seven guests had nothing to eat in the
  original morning spread.
