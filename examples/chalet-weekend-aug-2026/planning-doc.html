Prep schedule

Thursday

Costco, then Adonis, then Boucherie d'Orient for the taouk and kafta. The butcher opens at 9 AM and gets busy — phone that order ahead. Marinated meat holds overnight and the marinade keeps working. Buy the Sunday chicken fresh today too, and the herbs — parsley bought earlier will not survive to Sunday. Phone the Man'oushé order in today as well; 6 dozen needs real notice.

Friday morning

Collect the manakish on the way out — all three Laval locations open at 7 AM. Then, at home: marinate the Sunday chicken in olive oil, garlic, seven spice, salt, pepper and lemon zest only. No juice. Divide into four flat freezer bags and freeze solid — they double as cooler ice on the drive.

Friday ~7 PM — arrive & grill

Arrival is late, so dinner lands around 8:30–9. Put the dips, toum and pita out the moment you walk in — nobody should be waiting on the grill on an empty stomach. Taouk and kafta onto the BBQ, fattoush assembled while they cook. Manakish just go on a counter, bagged.

Saturday

Sunday

Fries

Serves 26 · shared, vegan-friendly

Frozen fries1 large bag (~2.25 kg)

Run the two air fryers and the oven in parallel, not in sequence.

  1. Air fryers take ~600 g each — half-full basket, shaken at halfway. 200 °C, 15–18 min. That's ~1.2 kg, out first and crispest.
  2. Oven takes the remaining ~1 kg on one sheet pan at 220 °C, ~25 min.
  3. Salt everything the second it comes out.

Don't do two air-fryer rounds back to back. Batch one goes cold while batch two cooks and half the table eats warm fries. All three at once lands everything within about five minutes.

Watch the breaker. Two air fryers are roughly 3000 W between them. On one chalet circuit with a kettle going, that trips. Separate outlets, ideally separate rooms.

No oil needed — frozen fries are already par-fried.

Sniyet djej w batata — Teta's version

Lebanese roast chicken & sliced potatoes · Serves 26 · boneless

Thin overlapping potato slices, whole garlic cloves, sliced onion, seven spice, olive oil, and enough lemon juice that it pools in the pan. Golden, not red. No grain underneath.

The four things that make it taste like hers:

1. Slice, don't wedge. ~5 mm rounds laid overlapping. The buried slices go soft and soaked; the exposed edges catch and darken. Wedges only ever do one or the other.

2. Serve it wet. ~2 cm of lemon, oil and water in the base, and it comes out with juices still pooling — spooned over the potatoes, mopped with bread. Not reduced to a glaze.

3. Whole garlic cloves, not just minced. Scattered between the slices they go sweet and soft, almost confit.

4. Covered most of the way, uncovered only at the end. Long enough to brown the chicken, not long enough to cook the pan dry.

Cook-day timeline — for a 3:00 PM meal

  1. 1:00 PM

    Preheat, slice Every oven to 200 °C / 400 °F. Potatoes ~5 mm rounds, onions thin.

  2. 1:15 PM

    Lemon on the chicken Squeeze 7 lemons over, massage through the bags, leave at room temperature.

  3. 1:25 PM

    Layer the pans Potato slices overlapping, onion scattered through, whole garlic cloves tucked between. Oil, salt and seven spice over. Pour in lemon juice and water — about 2 cm of liquid in the base.

  4. 1:35 PM

    Into the ovens, covered Foil on tight.

  5. 1:55 PM

    The handoff — set an alarm in every chalet Lift the foil, chicken on top in a single layer, skin-side up, all the marinade poured over. Do not stir the potatoes — the layering is the whole point. Foil back on.

  6. 2:20 PM

    Uncover — for colour only The single uncovered stretch. It's to brown the chicken, not to reduce the pan.

  7. 2:40 PM

    Temperature check 74 °C / 165 °F internal, potatoes tender, chicken browned. There should still be a proper layer of juices in the base. If the chicken colours too fast, foil back on.

  8. 2:45 PM

    Out and rest 10 minutes, loosely tented.

  9. 2:55 PM

    Finish Sumac, torn parsley, lemon wedges around the edges.

  10. 3:00 PM

    Serve Spoon the pan juices over. Toum in two bowls, bread on the table.

Don't reduce it at the end. The instinct on seeing liquid in the tray is to leave it in longer. Resist that — pull it while there's still a proper layer of juices. That's the dish.

This also solves the oven problem. About 70 minutes total, and a wet tray is far more forgiving: it doesn't punish you if the ovens run cold, the timing slips, or it sits fifteen minutes before everyone sits down. You no longer need to par-cook anything at home.

It also travels and reheats far better if you do cook ahead — the pan liquid protects the potatoes where dry roasted wedges go grainy. Hold back the sumac, parsley, lemon and toum until serving either way.

Vegan breakfast — one line item, don't cut it

Croissants contain butter and eggs are eggs. That leaves your 7 vegan guests with manakish and jam — genuinely fine as a breakfast, since the manakish are the main event anyway.

The one thing worth keeping is oat milk (4 L, $14). Without it they drink black coffee for three mornings, and that's a poor trade for fourteen dollars.

The manakish plan

5 dozen za'atar — 60 pieces. Across Saturday, Sunday and Monday that's about 20 a morning for 33 people, which works alongside eggs, fruit and labneh. Confirm the by-the-dozen price when you phone it in.

All-za'atar has a quiet advantage: it's vegan. Your 7 vegan guests eat the same breakfast as everyone else, which the original spread didn't allow.

Manakish is a same-day product. Bought Friday morning they're perfect Saturday and tired by Sunday.

No fridge, no freezer. There's nothing perishable in a za'atar manoushe — dough, olive oil, za'atar. Sealed at room temperature they hold three days comfortably, which covers Saturday through Monday.

Staling is the only enemy, and it's about air, not warmth. Keep the stacks tightly bagged from the moment you leave the bakery. An open bag on a counter goes dry and brittle by Sunday.

Oven, and it beats the air fryers here. You're warming about 20 a morning for 33 people, and an oven takes 10–12 at a time against 4 in a fryer basket. At 180 °C: wrapped in foil in a stack, 8–10 min for soft and pliable, the way they're eaten in Lebanon — or spread flat on sheet pans, 5 min for a crisper base.

Never microwave — the dough goes leathery. Save the air fryers for the Friday fries.

Buy two-thirds of the avocados firm and one-third ripe, or they all go at once. Apples and whole pineapple keep the entire stay without fridge space — the main reason they beat berries on a four-day trip.

60 eggs across three mornings is about 0.6 per person. Treat eggs as a Saturday and Sunday thing rather than a daily default.

The recipes

Aperol spritz — 3 parts Prosecco, 2 parts Aperol, splash of soda, over ice with an orange slice.
Tequila-kombucha — 2 oz blanco, 4 oz ginger or citrus kombucha, squeeze of lime, over ice.
Arak — 1 part arak, 2 parts cold water, then ice. In that order.

The arak order matters and people get it wrong. Pour the arak, add the water — it turns milky white, which is the point — and add ice last. Drop ice into neat arak and the anise oils crystallise on the surface of the cubes and the drink is ruined. Water first, always.

Arak belongs on the Friday table more than anywhere else: mezze, grilled meat, arak is the classic combination. Small glasses topped up often, not a tumbler.

Ice is the thing that actually runs out

Sixty kilos gets you through Friday and Saturday. Plan a restock Saturday at the store near the chalets — 100 spritz served over full glasses of ice eats through it faster than anyone expects, and there's no fixing it at 10 PM.

Check whether the chalets have icemakers before you buy; if two do, you need much less.

Equipment

  • 4 deep disposable foil roasting pans + 4 rimmed baking sheets to support them
  • Heavy-duty foil
  • Instant-read thermometer
  • ~32 skewers, if the taouk and kafta aren't pre-skewered
  • 4 heavy-duty freezer bags (2 L+)
  • 2 coolers
  • Grill basket for the vegan mushrooms
  • 2 air fryers — for the Friday fries

Chalet logistics

  • Four pans will not fit in one oven. Split across 2–3 chalets, one person per oven.
  • Preheat everything at the same time. Chalet ovens run cold and slow.
  • Don't crowd the chicken. Overlapping pieces steam instead of roasting.
  • Both quinoa salads go out Saturday, alongside the tacos. Sealed and cold until then. Leftovers work for late-night grazing and again Sunday — a cold grain salad holds fine once opened, and it's vegan as bought.
  • Name the Sunday cooks on Friday. A 1:00 PM start means someone is committed by lunchtime the morning after a party. Pick two, one per oven cluster.
  • Fridge space is the hidden constraint: 13.5 kg of meat, 4 kg of yogurt and 7 kg of dips. Map out which chalet fridge holds what before you unload.
  • Two air fryers on one circuit will trip a breaker. Separate outlets, ideally separate rooms.
  • The manakish need counter space, not fridge or freezer — keep the stacks bagged and out of the way.
